AONDI Research

Acousto-Optic Nondestructive Inspection | FEA & Experimental Validation

Project Overview

Contributed to AONDI (Acousto-Optic Nondestructive Inspection), an interdisciplinary research project aimed at detecting hidden structural defects in aerospace materials using acoustic excitation and optical imaging. The objective was to enable reliable nondestructive testing without disassembly or invasive inspection techniques.

I joined the project during Fall 2024 as the research team restarted with limited laboratory access. The team pivoted toward computational modeling and acoustic-based testing, focusing on validating finite element predictions through experimental vibration visualization on aluminum test plates.

Finite Element Analysis & Modal Simulation

Developed finite element models in SolidWorks to predict resonant frequencies and mode shapes for 6061 aluminum plates measuring 300 mm × 300 mm × 3 mm. Conducted modal and harmonic response studies across a 0–1000 Hz range, identifying a dominant resonance at approximately 315 Hz with less than 5% deviation from analytical calculations.

Addressed early mesh convergence issues that caused frequency variations of up to 20% by refining element density to 1 mm. The sensitivity of modal results to material properties reinforced the importance of accurate alloy selection for reliable simulation outcomes in defect detection research.

CAD Design & Experimental Apparatus

Designed parametric CAD assemblies of the complete experimental setup in SolidWorks, including aluminum test plates, bass driver mounting systems, fixtures, and camera positioning. Ensured geometric consistency between simulation models and physical hardware to allow meaningful comparison of predicted and observed vibration patterns.

Assisted with experimental integration tasks such as amplifier upgrades, mounting hardware installation, and camera alignment. Documented assembly constraints and procedures to support future project iterations and lab continuity.

Experimental Validation & Image Analysis

Conducted experimental trials by exciting aluminum plates at predicted resonant frequencies and visualizing vibration patterns using salt displacement methods. Captured optical data with high-resolution imaging to observe standing wave formations corresponding to modal predictions.

Implemented basic image processing techniques in Python, converting captured frames to grayscale and performing pixel-wise subtraction to isolate vibration patterns. Comparisons between processed images and FEA mode shapes confirmed detectable trends, while also identifying limitations caused by underpowered amplification hardware.

What I Learned

This project deepened my understanding of modal analysis and highlighted the critical role of mesh convergence in achieving accurate FEA results. I gained practical insight into how material properties influence simulation accuracy and how experimental limitations affect validation outcomes. Working in an interdisciplinary research environment reinforced the importance of documentation, iteration, and aligning computational models with real-world constraints.
